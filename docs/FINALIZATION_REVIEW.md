# Milestone 2.6 finalization review

This continues PR #4 on `feature/milestone-2-6-photorealism`. No replacement PR,
merge, rebase or Milestone 3 branch change is part of this work.

## Result

The character now uses the licensed middle-aged skin, high-poly eyes with a
visible iris/cornea layer, authored brows/lashes and short hair. Fabric and boot
normal/roughness responses are adjusted. The 52-bone rig and 21 clips remain.
Bone weights are normalized before mesh extraction, fixing holes in first-person
palms, wrists and cuffs. A dedicated regression preserves 15,334 arm-weighted
skin vertices. Both views retain the same physical carried object.

Animation cadence follows measured source stance speed, with phase-preserving
locomotion crossfades and reviewed cadence caps, avoiding a fast shuffle at
full controller speed. The old downward lantern hotspot is reduced. A small post-animation limb solver plants feet in support-
local coordinates (including moving cars), aligns both cargo grips, reaches
interaction targets and adjusts ladder hands to the existing rungs. It preserves
the animation's hand/boot orientation and never moves the player collider.

The locomotive's static rod pieces are replaced by quartered crank motion,
constant-length coupling/connecting rods and crossheads driven by actual train
speed. Three cab needles display speed and regulator/brake states. Printed
ranges and needle direction agree; no boiler-pressure system is invented.

Warehouse B gains buckled corrugated ceiling liners, selected missing glass
panes, broken braces/conduit, wall-edge debris and warning details. Close columns
are bolted I-sections; ladder rungs and gantry grating/rails follow the existing
traversal geometry. The skyline has continuous gabled roofs and shared masonry.
Dust uses soft translucent billboards. None of this dressing adds collision.

Flat bevelled surfaces now bake metre-scaled UVs and tangents once, sharing the
same PBR materials instead of sampling their maps triplanarly. Native export now
initializes the configured Vulkan baker, including materials generated by scripts. Scene-authored
views prepare destination-GPU pipelines at startup and preset changes behind a
loading panel, pausing/restoring play without visible camera cuts. A regression verifies camera/player/train
state preservation; route measurements wait until this preparation completes and
report its cost separately. High retains its
native resolution, 2× MSAA, SSAO and authored shadows. Low/Medium/High still change
presentation only. See the measured comparison below rather than assuming that
extra geometry or lower draw counts imply better performance.

## Visual evidence and review

[Seventeen matched three-way views](FINALIZATION_GALLERY.md) compare original
M2.5, previous M2.6 and finalization. [The new gameplay tour](recording/milestone26_finalization_tour.mp4)
uses actual movement/E/V inputs and labelled location cuts; it is not a cinematic
or a real-time FPS claim. [Capture method](recording/milestone26_finalization_tour.md).

The face and hands have authored anatomy and continuous skin, and the locomotive
mechanism is visibly connected during motion. The asset quality is improved but
not AAA photorealism: close facial expression remains neutral, hair cards can
alias, finger articulation uses authored arcs rather than per-finger contact,
and some distant shells/furniture remain simplified. The clothing remains the
licensed casual jacket and denim outfit with boots/belt, rather than a newly
sculpted railway uniform. Bare hands remain; no gloves or facial-animation system
are added in this pass. Warehouse damage is static
and repeated architectural modules remain visible. These limits are shown in
the ordinary front/side/rear, warehouse and distant views.

## Performance and Mac delivery

[Final measured performance](PERFORMANCE_FINALIZATION.md) separates software
Vulkan from hosted Apple Silicon. A physical 2020 M1 fullscreen result is still
unavailable; neither environment proves the 30 FPS Low target. The packaged
[manual benchmark procedure](M1_BENCHMARK.md) records actual resolution, preset,
frame distributions and hardware information, with repeated runs and interactive
play checks.

The final runtime's [run 34](https://github.com/oiwdis/last-train-out/actions/runs/43797534032)
passes all 196 native tests, universal export/bundle/ZIP integrity, 31 build-time
headless checks and 34 graphical checks. Fresh downloads pass 31 Intel headless
checks and both Apple Silicon launch modes. The ZIP contains 142 baked shader
cache files and all 334 required game files. Local extraction verifies both
Mach-O architectures and executable permissions; native CI verifies the ad-hoc
signature. The development build is **not notarized**.

Prepared entry-pan worst frames improve from 370–522 ms to 13–14 ms. However,
Warehouse B steady-state medians are 79% / 55% / 18% longer on Low/Medium/High
than the previous build in the hosted complete-build comparison. The final
Vulkan driver resolves the tested Metal library incompatibility; that portability
fix has a measurable cost here. The build-runner player-ready milestone is
59.4 seconds, including 22.5 seconds of view preparation. The fresh runner takes
longer. These results do not establish physical-M1 fullscreen playability.

The first fresh-download attempt also exposes a **Vulkan device-loss/GPU timeout**
when starting the packaged route diagnostic after its presets diagnostic passes.
The complete source comparison and ordinary packaged gameplay checks pass, but
this failed process is retained as a release concern. An independent runner
subsequently passes both launch modes and all six preset/three route cases using
the same ZIP. The timeout does not reproduce there; its cause remains unproven.
Both attempts are recorded, with latest exact-head checks linked from PR #4. MoltenVK cache-persistence messages and
Godot's export-time off-tree transform diagnostics are also retained in raw logs.

## Completion scope

The character/material pass, locomotion blending and support contacts, all three
bulky-object grips in both perspectives, separate first-person arms, animated
locomotive mechanism, functional gauge values, close station steelwork and
Warehouse B damage are implemented. The matched gallery and gameplay tour show
the result without hiding the remaining face, finger and distant-detail limits.
Existing gameplay is covered by all 187 original checks plus nine new checks.
Asset credits and the reproducible asset pipeline are updated.

The physical M1 performance target and the combined M3 danger-fog review remain
unverified because those environments are unavailable here. Intel graphical
rendering is also outside the available runner capability; native Intel
validation runs the packaged gameplay self-test headlessly. The native delivery
gate is reported separately above.

## Integration and recommendation

[Milestone 3 integration notes](MILESTONE3_INTEGRATION.md) identify likely conflicts
and stable presentation/player/train interfaces. Standard materials receive fog;
quality settings never change danger simulation. The combined M3 fog visual
review remains pending until the separate work is available.

**Recommendation: hold the merge.** The implementation and review evidence are
ready for evaluation, but the physical 2020 M1 fullscreen check is still needed
to accept the steady-state regression and startup cost. The packaged GPU-timeout
failure passed its independent rerun but still warrants observation;
passing functional tests alone is not a performance or visual sign-off. Keep
PR #4 unmerged, with the exact latest CI/build links in its description. Close-up
face/hair quality, finger/ladder stiffness, bare hands and distant simplification
remain explicit visual limitations rather than completed photorealistic polish.
