### Track, terrain and materials

The original baked Curve3D still determines train placement and travel distance. Each 120 m terrain section contains cross sections every 6 m. The inside 18 m follows the rail's local lateral axis. Farther out, widening runs toward world north and south, keeping distant rim vertices from folding over on a curve. Both neighboring chunks sample the same rim, including UV coordinates and elevation. All 43 chunks retain physical ground. Nearby visibility extends 520 m along the corridor and exceeds the 350 m review-camera range. The track shoulder provides standing room for moving ladder boarding. The drainage channel begins outboard of that shoulder.

The corridor ships as a compressed scene with mesh, collision, instance transforms and batch bounds baked together. A matching route geometry signature selects it during ordinary threaded mission loading. A custom route can rebuild its own geometry. A bulk transform buffer and explicit bounds preserve rail sleepers and vegetation on headless build machines. Ties, poles, fences and cutout foliage are batched or range-limited. An original four-species/variation vegetation atlas and seamless fog field supplement the existing licensed environment kit. Rob Tuytel's CC0 grass-and-rock scan supplies colour and relief. The asset manifest records the source files and digests. ### Fog and stations

The banks move with the existing route front. Individual layers have different widths, irregular heights and curved profiles. Their separation across 16 m intervals yields parallax as the player or train moves. Two mipmapped texture lookups drive broad changes in density and slower filaments. Distance fog attenuates the actual train, buildings and distant ground. Desaturation, light colour and sky response increase further inside. Low draws two layers, Medium four, and High six. Medium uses ordinary distance fog and these meshes. All share the same shader and existing material-preparation lifetime. High retains the integration's separately controlled volumetric setting.

Ashford's transfer gantry spans the loading pads. An elevated coal tipple, inclined conveyor, freight siding, box van and weathered containers identify the yard. Numbered bays stage uncollected crates. Tool storage, an overhead pipe and fitter's bench distinguish the workshop interior. Fixed architectural footprints share the station's original static collision body. Dressing may disappear at detail range independently of the permanent surfaces the player walks on.

Blackwater has crossing gates, painted stop lines, a main road, gabled houses, utility lights and a parked delivery van. An awning and window frames distinguish Crossing Stores. A second sign points to the evacuation register, and the track-crew shed identifies its coal reserve. Shallow concrete thresholds join the outdoor grade to the existing walk-through doorways.

## Inspection and validation method

The source baseline passed 305/305 on pinned Godot 4.5.1. Four additional scene inspections exercise the live mission. They check rail surface assignments and junction alignment, both directions through the masonry return, door and window structure, all 42 terrain joins, ground raycasts on both sides of bends, saved instances and bounds, fog mesh depth, texture assignments, station landmarks and physics ownership. The unchanged gameplay suites still exercise moving ladders, interiors, heavy cargo, rider restoration, saves and outcomes. [Scene inspections](../tests/integration/test_environment_overhaul.gd)

The matched Metal review uses the PR #6 build 104 download with ZIP SHA-256 `95d5ccbc65700a2d1f7b8e641eef3fd880f35b34221d09077ebd58a45a49b5ee`. One shared external camera script runs against each exported PCK, using the packaged Apple Silicon selector. A separate process captures each game at the same 16 coordinates, three presets and 960 × 540 window size. Camera, target, observer and danger-front coordinates are recorded and compared. The separate image passes remove the route and inventory panels and keep the warning overlay in fog views. Motion in individual wisps follows the running shader clock.

Timing passes keep the gameplay UI and disable VSync. Each pose records 60 entry intervals, 48 settling frames and 180 steady intervals, in two new processes per build with the order reversed for the repeat. Every measured interval is retained. Preset preparation, scene construction, first draw and ordinary cold/warm player launch use separate records. The selector-to-play test uses system audio. The fixed-view matrix uses the dummy audio driver. App shader caches are private per build and ordinary-player preset. The runner and its OS/driver caches are shared. GPU and CPU counters describe different parts of the pipeline and can overlap. Static engine memory excludes allocations in other libraries. RSS is polled independently.
